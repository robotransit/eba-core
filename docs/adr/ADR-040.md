# ADR-040: Drift Semantics and Epistemic Commitment

**Status:** Proposed (Target: v0.2.0 alignment)  
**Scope:** Agent Loop / Drift Monitoring (ADR-039 extension)  

---

## Objective

Define the epistemic semantics of drift detection and the permitted system responses to sustained drift within the ECK kernel.

This ADR resolves whether drift is treated as transient operational state or as persistent epistemic evidence, and establishes the constraints this imposes on kernel behavior.

---

## Context

The ECK architecture establishes the following core principles:

- The LLM is not a trusted authority surface  
- The kernel maintains an independent, deterministic epistemic record  
- Observability is primary; enforcement is mediated and explicit  
- Safety posture is monotonic (policy modes upgrade only)  
- Silent loss of information that affects system reasoning is prohibited  

Drift monitoring detects sustained divergence between predicted and observed outcomes. This signal represents a degradation in alignment between the agent’s internal model and actual execution results.

The question addressed by this ADR is:

> What is the kernel’s epistemic commitment when sustained drift is observed?

---

## Decision

### 1. Drift is Epistemic Evidence (Append-Only)

Drift is classified as **epistemic evidence**, not transient operational state.

- Drift observations MUST be preserved for the lifetime of the kernel execution epoch  
- Drift history MUST NOT be silently reset, truncated, or overwritten  
- Drift accumulation is part of the kernel’s audit surface  

---

### 2. Sustained Drift Triggers Monotonic Escalation

Sustained or severe drift MUST drive **one-way safety escalation**.

- Drift MAY influence policy mode transitions (e.g., NORMAL → GUIDED → ENFORCED → HALT)  
- Drift MUST NOT result in relaxation or downgrading of safety posture  
- Any escalation triggered by drift MUST be consistent with irreversible upgrade invariants  

---

### 3. Internal Drift Reset is Prohibited

The kernel MUST NOT perform silent or automatic reset of drift state.

- Resetting drift erases epistemic evidence and violates auditability  
- The kernel MUST NOT “recover” by discarding accumulated drift history  
- Any implementation that replaces or reinitializes drift state during a kernel execution epoch is non-compliant  

---

### 4. Recovery is External to the Kernel

Recovery from sustained drift is defined as an **external operation**, not an internal kernel behavior.

- Recovery MAY occur via supervised restart or reinitialization of the agent  
- Such operations MUST define a new kernel execution epoch  
- Pre-restart drift history MUST remain preserved and accessible for audit  

The kernel’s role is to **surface and preserve drift**, not to rehabilitate itself.

---

### 5. Observability Requirements

Drift-related events MUST be observable and auditable.

- The **agent loop (ADR-039)** is the canonical point for logging drift-triggered decisions and escalation events  
- DriftMonitor is responsible for signal accumulation only and MUST NOT perform logging with behavioral implications  
- Logging MUST include:
  - Drift accumulation signals (as defined by DriftMonitor)
  - Threshold crossings or instability classifications
  - Any escalation decisions influenced by drift  

These logs MUST NOT introduce behavioral side-effects.

---

## Implications

### Architectural

- Drift monitoring becomes an **append-only evidence subsystem**  
- Aligns drift handling with existing monotonic safety and authority constraints  
- Reinforces the separation between kernel observation and external control decisions  

---

### Implementation

- Any current logic that resets or reinitializes drift state MUST be removed or revised  
- DriftMonitor implementations MUST preserve historical state across cycles  
- Agent loop (ADR-039) MUST treat drift as cumulative input to escalation logic  

---

### Operational

- Long-running deployments MUST rely on external supervision for recovery  
- Kernel instances are expected to halt or harden under sustained drift rather than self-reset  
- Audit trails become first-class artifacts for post-hoc analysis and intervention  

---

## Non-Goals

This ADR does NOT:

- Define the specific thresholds or metrics used to classify drift  
- Mandate a particular DriftMonitor implementation  
- Introduce any new authority coupling between drift and execution behavior beyond policy mediation  

---

## Summary

Drift is a first-class epistemic signal.  
It is **preserved, accumulated, and acted upon via escalation**, not erased.

The kernel observes and records.  
Recovery is external.
